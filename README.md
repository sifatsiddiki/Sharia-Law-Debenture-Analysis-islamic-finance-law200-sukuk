An academic law assignment on Islamic finance mechanisms, analyzing how debentures function under Sharia law through Sharia-compliant instruments like Sukuk in Saudi Arabia and Malaysia. Developed for LAW200 (North South University).
# Who are the Debenture Holders in Countries That Follow Sharia Law?

##  Project Overview
This repository hosts an academic assignment written for the **Legal Environment of Business (LAW200)** course. The paper explores how traditional debt frameworks (debentures) are fundamentally transformed into Sharia-compliant instruments (**Sukuk**) in jurisdictions that restrict interest or *Riba*, such as **Saudi Arabia** and **Malaysia**.

### Key Frameworks & Tenets Analyzed
1. **The Prohibition of Riba (Interest):** Sharia law explicitly forbids fixed interest rates. Hence, open-constrained companies structure borrowing costs by linking returns directly to organizational performance, profit-sharing, and operational risk.
2. **Money as a Medium, Not a Product:** Analyzing the core rule that forbids treating money as a tradeable commodity, steering investors toward speculative and unique asset-based opportunities instead.
3. **Comparative Case Studies:**
   * **Saudi Arabia:** How Islamic banks leverage Sukuk to grant investors partial ownership of tangible projects or assets, overseen by the Capital Market Authority (CMA).
   * **Malaysia:** Evaluation of asset-backed versus asset-based Sukuk models, regulated thoroughly under Malaysia's Securities Commission.
4. **Risk Profile:** Sukuk/debenture holders retain limited liability, assuming risks bounded tightly to their initial configurations while issuers mitigate structural project overheads.
